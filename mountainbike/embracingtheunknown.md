# Embracing the Unknown

![Discovering a new trail in Menorca](/assets/unknown/DSC09222.jpeg)
Words & Photos: **Nicolas Brunner (photos are self-shot)**

Most of us ride the same trails everyday. Some will even go as far as claiming that their local trails are the best. After all you know every secret little path, side hit and exact speed that’s needed to clear features. And best of all: No need to prepare anything before going on a ride. You can just switch off your brain, get out and enjoy the ride. We get joy from pushing ourselves, clearing a feature for the first time or just finding flow on a trail we know by hearth.

And that’s all well and good. 

But do you remember the excitement and thrill when you discovered those trails for the first time? Just heading out to the woods without clear plan and taking the first trail entry that looks promising. The excitement of what is yet to discover, before you start building up habits. Those are the moments I enjoy most in life - where my inner curiosity is in the lead and I just get out and explore. Sometimes you get rewarded with incredible singletrack, sometimes you end up pushing your bike through the bushes more than actually riding. But it doesn’t matter. Because it’s all about the joy of exploring and the excitement of the unknown ahead.

And while it’s hard to maintain this feeling on your local trails, it’s actually incredibly easy to trigger it wherever you go. Just bring a bike along.

![Bikes inside our camper van](/assets/unknown/DSC08530.jpeg)
*Exploring countries by bicycle is my favorite way of travel. It doesn’t always need to be bikepacking - just bring your bike along.*

This spring we took the chance to travel 2 months with our little family, before our son would start his school career in summer. We loaded our camper van and headed south to the balearic islands for a few weeks of sun, food, sea, mountains and dolce far niente. For me as a first time visiter of Mallorca, I was absolutely blown away at how popular it is for road cycling. There is a very strong cycling culture there and it’s rare you’ll see a stretch of road without a group of cyclists. And it’s not hard to understand why: Perfect weather conditions most of the year, fantastic landscapes and a vast network of small side roads.

![Pulling our kid up the hill](/assets/unknown/IMG_9272.jpeg)
*Making the climbs enjoyable for our 4-year old.*

And while I can also enjoy this side of cycling, this is not really where my hearth will sparkle most. I wanted to explore the island from a mountainbiker’s perspective. Obviously, with the trip being a family vacation, this would mostly be restricted to late evening or early morning rides. 

Whenever we were in a new spot, I would check the topographic map, study the terrain, and look for small paths nearby, before settling on a rough route. As this strategy embraces the unknown, there’s always the chance that you’ll end up hiking up some sections or - ghasp - walking down. This is especially true if you’re in a foreign country, where you neither know the terrain nor the map data well.

Of course, you could also rely on Trailforks or Strava Heatmaps to find some rideable trails. But in my experience, the best trails are often not featured there. And then there’s also the excitement of getting out and exploring the unknown that gets lost when preparing everything perfectly in advance. You could even argue, that the constant availability of information is detrimental to our real world experience. Knowing in advance, what we will experience takes the thrill out.

This naiveness and eagerness to explore will of course also cause some friction. On my first two rides on Mallorca I found exactly zero rideable trails. The first evening, I would ride up a small road up to an old sanctuary in the hope that I would somehow find a trail down the other side. Hope was a bad advisor in that case and I ended up descending the same road I had climbed. My second ride was even worse. According to the map, there should have been a path to the top of a hill, where you should then be able to follow the ridge until the bottom. In reality, there was no path to the top. I still  decided to give it a try, hefted my bike onto my back and climbed the 400 meters of altitude through bushes and sharp rocks until the top. As it turned out, there was also no path to ride down. So I was back at walking down the route I had climbed. This was really off to a bad start.

![Fireroad climb in Mallorca](/assets/unknown/DSC08445.jpeg)
*The closest I came to mountainbiking during my first two rides in Mallorca.*

On my third ride, I finally found a few meters of rideable trail, but it was far from mountainbiker’s paradise. But slowly things began to click together. We were in the incredibly beautiful mountains of Mallorca on the north part of the island. I wanted to try riding one of the main peaks of the island. Here at least I found some forum entries from hikers, so I knew there was at least a path. To my surprise, the first part of the climb was rideable. Then once again, it became a hike-a-bike. I don’t really mind, I like the slow pace that comes with it as it let’s you explore line options for the fun part later. An hour after I had started, the sun started to rise from the sea and painted the island in some fantastic orange light. 

![Sunrise over Mallorca](/assets/unknown/IMG_9366.jpeg)
*Sunrise over Mallorca and the mediterranean sea. Early morning rides are the best!*

There is something really peaceful about early morning rides where you are out alone in nature and experiencing the rise of a new day. Shortly before reaching the peak I decided to leave my bike behind and continue without it. The rock formations were littered with unrideable holes and it became a mix of climbing and hiking. After taking in the views and being able to overlook the entire island, I headed back down to grab my bike. I quickly realized that luckily, this was a day where I would easily find flow. Not flow in the sense of what many of us would call a flow trail. This wasn’t terrain that called for speed. But rather a level of flow reached by connecting with the trail and the challenges it throws at you.

![Mountainbiking in Mallorca](/assets/unknown/DSC08671.jpeg)
*Hiking paths are a mountainbiker’s best friend on Mallorca - you won’t find any dedicated trails.*

Back at the camper van, dopamine levels were still high. It’s funny how fast you forget past defeats under such circumstances. Those are the experiences I cannot get enough of. When the uncertainty is high, the reward is even bigger and those are the rides that will stay in memory for a long time.

![Mountainbiking in Menorca](/assets/unknown/DSC09188.jpeg)
*This was one of my favorite randomly discovered trails on Menorca, Mallorca’s smaller sister island. It isn’t featured on any sort of map.*

I continued finding both, frustrating hike-a-bikes and fantastic trails along our trip. Back on the Spanish mainland, we travelled through Catalonia and towards the Pyrenees mountain range. Exploring terrain there became less of a gamble. I also spent half a day in the well-known trail network of Santa Coloma de Farners. Riding on these rock slabs was a fantastic experience. It’s also easily accessible because the trails are well documented in Trailforks or Strava. On the flipside, I also find it less thrilling because it doesn’t make my little inner explorer shine.

![Trail in the Pyrenees](/assets/unknown/IMG_9639.jpeg)
*A beauty of a trail at the foot of the Pyrenees right at the French and Spanish border. Unfortunately this region was savaged by wildfires during a very dry month of July just one month later.*

Discovering new trails and regions by intuition rather than relying on the available information on the internet might seem like a stupid idea at first glance. You will find yourself questioning *what the fuck am I doing here?* more often than not. But when your efforts get rewarded with a ride you did not expect but dreamt off, all of those thoughts fade into insignificance. In that sense: Just to get out and explore.

All of this is obviously not only restricted to our funny little mountainbiking world. So, may I propose to take a minute and ask yourself - what motivates you in your everyday life and in mountainbiking specifically?
